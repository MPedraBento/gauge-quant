# GaugeQuant: Theoretical Foundations & Mathematical Blueprint

## 1. The Core Objective
Standard post-training quantization (PTQ) fails on Large Language Models because of massive, structural activation outliers. These outliers are generated by upstream projection matrices (e.g., $W_{up}$, $W_V$). Min-Max quantizers clip these features, destroying the linguistic manifold. GaugeQuant solves this by learning a **Continuous Block-Diagonal Gauge Symmetry** to democratize variance *before* quantization.

## 2. Continuous Gauge Symmetry
In a standard LLM boundary, hidden activations $H$ are produced by an upstream projection (e.g., $H = X_{in} W_{up}$) and passed to a downstream projection ($W_{down}$). We inject an orthogonal matrix $R \in SO(d)$ and its transpose:
$$Y = (H \cdot R)(R^T \cdot W_{down}) \equiv H \cdot W_{down}$$
Because $R \cdot R^T = I$, the mathematical output of the network remains strictly invariant. We merely shift the geometric basis of the intermediate space $H$ to one that is hardware-friendly.

> **Critical invariant: R never rotates the layer input X.**
> The gauge acts exclusively on *internal boundary representations* between paired projections:
> - **Attention**: R lives between the V output and O input: $(X W_V) \cdot R \cdot R^T \cdot W_O$
> - **MLP**: R lives between the SwiGLU output H and down_proj: $H \cdot R \cdot R^T \cdot W_{down}$
>
> The input activations X flowing into a transformer block are never touched by R. This is what makes the transformation a true gauge symmetry — it is invisible to the rest of the network.

## 3. The Cayley Transform (Stiefel Manifold Optimization)
Standard gradient descent destroys orthogonality ($R^T R \neq I$). To optimize $R$ safely over the Stiefel manifold, we learn a skew-symmetric matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ where $A = -A^T$. 
We map $A$ to $SO(d)$ via the **Cayley Transform**:
$$R = (I - A)(I + A)^{-1}$$
**Critical Engineering Constraint:** The matrix inversion $(I + A)^{-1}$ and the rotation mapping must strictly be computed in `float32` to prevent the catastrophic mantissa drift inherent to `bfloat16`.

## 4. The Dual-Graph Optimization Topology
To find the optimal $R$ that flattens outliers without destroying the LLM's intelligence, we execute two mathematically isolated optimization graphs simultaneously.

### Graph A: The Brain (Cross-Entropy)
The base LLM weights ($W$) are updated using standard Next-Token Prediction (Cross-Entropy). Because the forward pass is mathematically invariant, Graph A is strictly blind to the Geometer's rotations.

### Graph B: The Geometer (Gauge Loss)
We compute a geometric proxy penalty (LogSumExp, a differentiable approximation of the $L_\infty$ norm) on the rotated space $Z = H \cdot R$. 
$$\mathcal{L}_{gauge} = \log \left( \sum \exp(|Z_i|) \right)$$

**The Gradient Firewall (`.detach()`):**
If $\mathcal{L}_{gauge}$ flows backward into previous layers, it forces the upstream weight matrices to rewrite their intelligence to satisfy the penalty. We sever the graph via `H.detach()` before applying $R$. This one-way mirror ensures gradients from $\mathcal{L}_{gauge}$ strictly update $R$ and nothing else.
